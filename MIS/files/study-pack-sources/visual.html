1. Porter's five forces (Ch 3)

Profit = one pizza; each force takes a slice. Strong force = less profit. Example: a cafe in Dhaka.

New entrants ↓

Means: new firms may join your market.

Strong when: profits are high; easy and cheap to start (low barriers to entry); customers not loyal.

Cafe: a new cafe opens next door with lower prices.

IT fights it: a system that is costly or hard to copy (app + loyalty data) = a barrier to entry.

Supplier power →

Means: firms that sell you your inputs (the "market of inputs").

Strong when: only 1-2 suppliers; strong brand; no other input you can use.

Cafe: only two coffee-bean importers, so they set the price.

IT fights it: online buying from many suppliers; SCM.

Rivalry (CENTRE)

Means: the fight between firms already in the market.

Why centre: it is the main force.

Strong when: many similar firms; price wars; big ads.

Cafe: five cafes on one street cut prices.

IT fights it: use IS for a strategy (sheet 2).

← Buyer power

Means: your customers (the "market of outputs").

Strong when: easy to switch; prices compared online; few big buyers.

Cafe: customers compare prices on Foodpanda.

IT fights it: loyalty app / CRM, so leaving costs them points.

Substitutes ↑

Means: a different product that meets the same need.

Strong when: many cheaper options; easy to switch.

Cafe: tea stall, energy drink, coffee made at home.

IT fights it: a unique product + switching costs.

Exam line: The intensity of the five forces decides how profitable the industry is.
How to rate a force: ask "Can it push my price down or my cost up?" Yes, easily = strong.
Trap: Rival = same product, another cafe. Substitute = different product, same need (tea). New entrant = a cafe not open yet.
Airline: rivalry, suppliers, buyers HIGH; entrants LOW-MED; substitutes MED → low profit.

2. Four ways to win: generic strategies (Ch 3)

Forces = the danger. Strategies = how you win anyway. Hook: Cheapest - Different - Narrow - Close.

$ CHEAPEST = Low-cost leadership

Idea: be the cheapest, keep quality and service.

IS helps: cut waste: automatic restocking, efficient logistics.

Example: Walmart, Asda, easyJet.

Beats: powerful buyers and price wars (rivals avoid fighting the cheapest).

* DIFFERENT = Product differentiation

Idea: offer something unique others cannot copy easily.

IS helps: new products, better customer experience.

Example: Apple iPhone, Google.

Beats: substitutes and buyer power (loyal buyers do not switch).

◎ NARROW = Focus on market niche

Idea: serve one small market better than anyone.

IS helps: data on that customer group to target it well.

Example: Hilton Hotels; Amazon Prime (slide MCQ).

Beats: rivals (they cannot meet the niche's needs).

♥ CLOSE = Customer and supplier intimacy

Idea: strong ties so customers and suppliers stay.

IS helps: CRM, SCM, loyalty programs raise switching costs.

Example: Tesco, Amazon, DHL.

Beats: buyer power and substitutes (leaving costs them).

4. Six business objectives: why firms buy IS (Ch 1)

Hook: "Our New Customers Increase Company Survival"
O

Operational excellence

Same work, faster and cheaper.

Ex: Walmart; UPS ORION saves ~100 million miles a year.

N

New products, services, models

New things to sell, new ways to earn.

Ex: iPhone + iTunes; Netflix.

C

Customer and supplier intimacy

Know them well, so they stay.

Ex: hotels remember each guest's preferences.

I

Improved decision making

Decide with real-time data, not guesses.

Ex: Verizon's real-time dashboard.

C

Competitive advantage

Better than rivals: price, quality, speed.

Ex: Apple, Walmart, UPS.

S

Survival

You must have it to stay in business.

Ex: after Citibank's ATMs, every bank needed ATMs.

Trap: survival vs competitive advantage. Everyone has it and you need it to stay alive = survival. It puts you ahead = competitive advantage.

+ Help: work from anywhere, real-time across places, less travel, screen sharing
