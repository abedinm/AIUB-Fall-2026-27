Rapid facts: Ch 3 one-liners for MCQs

  • Intensity of the five forces decides industry profitability. Rivalry sits in the centre: it is the main force.
  • Barrier to entry = anything that makes starting expensive or hard, e.g. an IS that is costly to copy.
  • Switching costs = what it costs a customer to leave. High switching costs weaken buyers and substitutes.
  • Internet: lowers entry barriers, adds substitutes, raises buyer power, makes rivalry fiercer. Hit travel agencies, encyclopedias, newspapers.
  • Network economics: each new user adds value at near-zero extra cost (eBay, Facebook).
  • Virtual company: uses networks to link people, assets and ideas across firms (outsourcing production).
  • Business ecosystem: keystone firms build the platform (Apple iOS, Microsoft); niche firms build on it (app developers).
  • Synergy: outputs of some units feed others, or firms pool markets: costs fall (JP Morgan Chase mergers).
  • Core competency: what a firm is world-class at; IS that share knowledge strengthen it.
  • Scalability: more customers without costs rising in proportion (software, online stores, franchising).

Answer key: Parts B and C

Part B model answers

B1. A substitute uses a different technology to meet the same need; more substitutes push prices and margins down. Example: tea instead of Starbucks coffee.

B2. Rivalry among existing competitors is the main determinant of how competitive an industry is. The other four forces all act on the firms already competing, so it sits in the centre.

B3. IS such as CRM and SCM build strong ties: they record customer preferences, personalize offers and link suppliers' systems to the firm. This raises switching costs, so customers and suppliers stay. Example: Amazon's recommendations; Tesco's loyalty card.

B4. A value chain is one firm's activities in a line (primary and support), used to find where IS adds most value. A value web is many independent firms that use IT to coordinate their value chains and serve a market together; it is more flexible and customer-driven.

B5. Scalability is the ability of a business or system to grow to meet demand without costs rising in proportion, so margins hold or improve. Example: an app or online store adding thousands of users at almost no extra cost.
